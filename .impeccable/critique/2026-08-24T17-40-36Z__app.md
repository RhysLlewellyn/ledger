## Design Specificity Verdict

**Not category-interchangeable, but genre-generic rather than product-specific.**

There are no cards, no shadows, no rounded corners, no gradients, no tinted status pills. The one place a data colour touches text is `--color-data-neg` on negative movement amounts, and `globals.css` reasons about that exception ahead of time. The brief was honoured with real discipline.

But swap the nouns and this same composition, type system and rule vocabulary is a fund factsheet, an ONS release, a clinical-trial report. It reads "statistical yearbook" generically. Nothing in it reads "subscription billing" particularly.

**Structural sameness is the weakest part of the art direction.** All five routes are the same object: top rule → serif `h1` at `text-2xl` → 40–60 word standfirst → rule → content. `app/page.tsx:62` and `app/cohorts/page.tsx:39` are the same twelve lines with different words. The customer detail page — the one screen about a single account's life — is three consecutive tables in identical treatment.

**The biggest missed opportunity is the product's own name.** A ledger has facing debit and credit columns, a carried-forward line, and a double rule under a closing total. The build has the running balance right there (`running_pence`, signed `amount_pence`, a `kind` column) and does nothing with it. The single most on-brief, on-name device available — the accountant's double rule — appears nowhere.

**The type has no scale.** The largest thing on any page is 24px. The hero figure `£3,439,147` is `text-2xl`, the same size as the word "Overview". Assessment B measured six distinct font sizes across the whole product, with 1,989 of 2,013 text-bearing elements at 14px or 12px. A printed statistical yearbook will set one figure at 60pt and let it own the spread. Colour was banned; scale was not. The direction got the restraint right and then applied restraint to the one axis still available.

## Overall Impression

This is a confident, disciplined build that knows exactly what it is refusing to do, and it is stronger on the things nobody checks than on the things everybody does. The chart captions argue with their own headline metric. The empty state reconstructs the active predicate into English. The movement chart survives being printed in black and white. That is real craft and it is rare.

Where it falls down is the gap between what it claims and what it does. Two routes throw a raw framework exception when the database is asleep — including the one most worth linking to. A cohort grid whose source comment says "the shade carries the shape" renders as a near-uniform slab. An accessibility sweep reporting zero violations across seven pages is measuring a horizontal-scroll condition the stylesheet has made impossible to fail, while six chart labels sit clipped and unreachable at 360px.

**The single biggest opportunity:** the product is called Ledger and never once draws one.

## What's Working

**Findings-as-captions, computed from the data.** `app/charts/figure.tsx` makes every chart four things at once — a picture, a `<figcaption>` stating the conclusion, an `aria-label` describing the shape, and the numbers in a real `<table>` behind a native `<details>`. It works because it converts a chart from something the reader must interpret into something already interpreted, and because the sentence is computed it cannot quietly stop being true the way a hand-written caption does.

**Four-encoding, greyscale-first movement chart.** Position for direction, length for amount, SVG `<pattern>` for series, hue last. The brief said "printed report", and this is the element that actually behaves like one: print it in black and white and nothing is lost. It happens also to be the correct accessibility answer, which is what makes it read as authored rather than compliant.

**The empty state.** It doesn't say "no results" — it reconstructs the active predicate into English ("MRR is at least £9,999,999") and offers a single undo. It converts a dead end into a diagnosis with a named cause, and it is the clearest evidence in the build that someone thought past the happy path.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] Two of four routes have no database-failure fallback, and they are the linkable ones

`app/page.tsx` and `app/cohorts/page.tsx` each wrap their queries in try/catch and render a written explanation when Neon is asleep. **`app/customers/page.tsx` and `app/customers/[slug]/page.tsx` have zero try blocks, and there is no `error.tsx` anywhere in the app.** I verified this independently of Assessment A.

**Why it matters:** the README's own framing is that this is opened from a cold email against a free tier that suspends compute when idle. A technical director clicking through to `/customers` on a cold instance gets Next's raw "Application error: a server-side exception has occurred" — the worst possible first impression, on the most impressive page, in precisely the scenario the build was designed for.

**Fix:** add `app/error.tsx` rendering the message that already exists twice, and lift the duplicated `Unavailable` component out of `app/page.tsx` into a shared module so all four routes share one copy.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] Three accessibility defects the clean sweep missed

The sweep reports zero axe violations across seven pages. Three real failures sit underneath that:

1. **The cohort grid cannot be reached by keyboard.** `app/charts/cohort-grid.tsx:51` is `<div className="mt-6 overflow-x-auto">` wrapping a table with **no links, no buttons and no `tabindex`**. A keyboard-only user who is not running a screen reader has nothing to Tab to inside that region and cannot scroll it horizontally at all — columns 12 to 23 are unreachable. The customer table escapes this only incidentally, because every row happens to contain a link.
2. **Six chart labels are clipped and unreachable at 360px.** Assessment B measured `/` at a document `scrollWidth` of 518px against a 360px viewport, with six synthesized wheel gestures moving `scrollX` by zero — `html { overflow-x: clip }` holds. The consequence is not scrolling, it is clipping: the x-axis month labels `Feb 2026`, `May 2026` and `Jul 2026` overflow with **no scrollable ancestor**, on both charts. The `<ol>` sits outside the `overflow-x-auto` that wraps the plot. The source comment says the every-third-month thinning exists because "twenty-four labels do not fit at 360px" — the thinned set still does not fit.
3. **Fifty row links are 18px tall** on `/customers` at 360px, against WCAG 2.5.8's 24x24. This was a deliberate call in an earlier pass; it is still a call worth revisiting now that everything else in the build clears it. (B correctly adjudicated the skip link's 1x1 measurement as a false positive — it measures 134x41 on focus.)

**Why it matters:** the outreach leans on accessibility as the quality proof, and the sweep's `documentScrollsSideways` check is tautological — it tests `scrollWidth > innerWidth`, and `overflow-x: clip` pins `scrollWidth` to `clientWidth` by definition. Both assessments arrived at that independently. The check cannot fail, so it has been passing without meaning anything.

**Fix:** `tabindex="0"` plus an accessible name on the cohort scroller; move the axis `<ol>` inside the scrolling container so the labels travel with the plot; replace the sideways-scroll probe with one that measures overflow against a scrollable ancestor; re-run the sweep at 1024 and 360, not only 1280.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable audit`

### [P1] Four thousand customers and no way to search by name — and the 404 promises one

`app/customers/filters.tsx` offers plan, status, channel, country, signup date and MRR range. There is **no text input** anywhere on the page; I confirmed zero `type="search"` or `type="text"` in the served markup. To find one named account you sort by name and page through up to 80 pages, two clicks at a time, because pagination is Previous/Next only.

**Why it matters:** finding a specific customer is the most common thing anyone does with a customer table. Worse, `app/not-found.tsx` sends the user to a link labelled **"Search the customer table →"**, which lands on a page with no search. That is a promise the product does not keep, in its own error state.

**Fix:** one text input filtering on name, in the same GET form, as one more URL parameter — it costs nothing the URL contract doesn't already give you. Expose `perPage`, which already exists in `CustomerQueryOptions` and is never surfaced. Add first/last page links.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable shape`

### [P1] Nothing acknowledges any navigation

No `loading.tsx`, no Suspense boundary, no transition, no pending state on Apply. Every sort header, filter submit, pager link and row link is a hard document navigation with no feedback.

**Why it matters:** this is Nielsen #1 and it applies to every interaction on the surface. Warm it reads as instant; cold it reads as broken. A power user clicks "MRR" to re-sort, sees nothing for two seconds, clicks again — and toggles the direction back.

**Fix:** `app/loading.tsx` rendering the page furniture — rules, `h1`, standfirst — with the data region left as ruled empty space. That is exactly what a printed report looks like before the figures are set, so it costs the direction nothing. Add `aria-busy` on `<main>` and a pressed state on Apply.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P2] The cohorts page is the weakest execution of the strongest idea

`cohort-grid.tsx` claims "the number carries the value and the shade carries the shape". The ramp is anchored 0–100% while every observed value sits between 59% and 100%, so intensity spans only 0.45–0.70 and the grid renders as a near-uniform slab. B measured 27 continuous `color-mix()` steps doing that work. There is no legend. And the explanatory caption is a `<caption>` *inside* the table, therefore inside the `overflow-x-auto` wrapper — so at 360px the prose is trapped in the data scroller and clips mid-sentence at "…who signed up in one m".

**Why it matters:** this is the page a design director is most likely to screenshot, and it currently shows a flat rectangle whose shading carries none of the shape its own source comment promises. On mobile a reader cannot finish the paragraph explaining what they are looking at without horizontally scrolling a retention grid.

**Fix:** anchor the ramp to the observed min/max, or use a two-stop ramp with a break at the median; add a small ramp key beside the offset header row; move the caption out of the `<table>` into a sibling `<p>` above the scroller, keeping the semantic link with `aria-describedby`.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable colorize`

## Persona Red Flags

Data-heavy analytics surface, so: **Alex** (impatient power user) and **Sam** (screen reader and keyboard only).

**Alex — impatient power user**

- No name search on 4,000 rows, and the 404 tells him there is one.
- Pagination is Previous/Next only. Page 40 costs 39 clicks. He will hand-edit `?page=40` — a workaround he invented, not a feature offered.
- Filters don't apply until he scrolls past 28 controls to an Apply button. Three refinements is three full scroll-and-submit cycles, with no live preview of what a tick would do.
- `perPage` exists in the options type and is never exposed. He wants 200 rows; the interface has the parameter and won't show him the knob.
- **"← All customers" destroys his view.** He filters to 117 UK/Enterprise/Active sorted by MRR, opens one, clicks the page's own back link, and lands on unfiltered 4,000. The state was in the URL and the interface threw it away.
- Charts have no hover readout. Every exact value costs opening a `<details>` and scanning a 24-row table.
- **The net-movement bars under the MRR line have no y-axis at all.** The £4m…£0 labels belong to the line plot above. The bars also hang *below* the £0 gridline, which reads as negative revenue in a chart whose own caption says movement was positive in every month.
- Zero keyboard shortcuts.

**Sam — screen reader and keyboard only**

Credit first, because this is well above the usual standard: skip link with `tabIndex={-1}` on `<main>`, `aria-sort` on the sorted column only, the caption built as one interpolated string to survive Chrome's whitespace-node collapse, facet counts moved from label to `aria-describedby`, native `<details>`, one global `:focus-visible`, real `<th scope>` on every table. What still breaks:

- **The nav announces three identical links and never says which page he is on.** `aria-current` appears zero times in the served HTML of all three destinations — verified directly. There is no visual current state either, so this fails everyone; for Sam it is total.
- **The filter form is not a landmark.** `<form method="get">` with no `aria-label` never becomes a `form` landmark, so he cannot reach the filters by rotor — he walks from the "Filters" `h2` every time.
- **`metadata.title` is the static "Customers" for every filtered view.** B confirmed `/customers` is always `Customers — Ledger`. So the first thing he hears after submitting a filter is the same title as before, then `h1`, then a 34-word standfirst about Postgres, and only then "117 customers match". A dynamic title puts the answer in the first two seconds for free.
- **He cannot reach the cohort grid at all.** See P1 above.
- The cohort ramp has no legend, so the one visually-encoded piece of information has no text equivalent. The printed percentage saves it — which also concedes the shade is carrying nothing.

## Minor Observations

- **The 404's `<title>` is `Ledger`, identical to the homepage.** In a tab strip or history, an error is indistinguishable from the front door.
- **Facet counts are global and go stale the instant a filter is applied.** `planFacets()` and `countryFacets()` take no arguments — verified. On the filtered screenshot the result line correctly says "117 customers match all 3 filters applied" while the panel two inches below still reads Enterprise 372 and United Kingdom 1,500. On a build that polices exactly this class of dishonesty elsewhere, it is the one place the interface asserts something untrue.
- **The favicon is a four-bar blue bar chart** — the generic analytics mark, on a build whose own stylesheet comment disparages "the generated-dashboard signature".
- **The movement chart's legend swatches don't match its bars.** The patterns are `patternUnits="userSpaceOnUse"` inside a `preserveAspectRatio="none"` SVG, so the diagonal renders at ~34° in the stretched plot and 45° in the legend. The legend is the mechanism that makes the greyscale encoding work, and it shows a different hatch from the thing it identifies.
- `mrrMin > mrrMax` is accepted and silently returns nothing, landing the user in an empty state with two clauses that contradict each other.
- Five data colours are declared; two of the four content screens use only `--color-data-1`. The full palette appears on exactly one chart.
- The y-axis label at £1,000,000 sits on the line's starting point; the `bg-(--color-paper)` occlusion leaves the line peeking out from under it.
- Two near-identical `Figure` components exist in `app/page.tsx` and `app/customers/[slug]/page.tsx`, differing by one type-size step and a note colour.
- `llms.txt` and the Agentic Browsing Lighthouse column are a genuinely current 2026 signal worth keeping visible.

## Questions to Consider

1. **The product is called Ledger and never once draws a ledger.** What if the customer detail page stopped being three generic tables and became a *statement* — facing debit and credit columns, the accountant's double rule under the closing balance, "carried forward" between pages? Every ingredient is already in the data. Would that one change do more for design specificity than everything else on this list combined?
2. **The ban was on colour, not on scale.** Why is `£3,439,147` set at the same size as the word "Overview"? What is the largest that number could be before this stops looking like a report — and is the current answer restraint, or nerve?
3. **`cohort-grid.tsx` says "the shade carries the shape."** Look at the render and say honestly whether it carries any. If anchoring the ramp to the observed range is what makes that sentence true, why was it anchored at 0 — and what else in this build is a well-argued comment describing something the render does not do?
4. **Every standfirst explains the engineering.** If the reader were a billing analyst who already trusts it, what would those 40–60 words become — and would a technical director think less of the build for cutting them, or more?
5. **Every state change is a full document navigation with no feedback, and the README treats that as a feature.** Is that a principle, or a constraint promoted to a principle? What is the smallest amount of JavaScript that buys the operator a pending state without giving up anything the URL contract currently provides?
