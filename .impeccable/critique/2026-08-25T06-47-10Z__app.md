## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment.** The surface treatment is unmistakably this product's; the composition is category-default with the paper skin applied. One screen of four is designed for what Ledger *is*.

Authored, and not liftable into another analytics product: the customer statement (facing Debit/Credit columns, a running balance, a `<tfoot>` labelled *Carried forward* under `border-style: double`); the favicon, a ruled page rather than the four-bar chart every competitor ships; three rule tokens split by job rather than by shade; the loading skeleton drawn as ruled paper at the real row height; pattern fills on the movement bars so hue is the fourth encoding rather than the first; the cohort ramp anchored to observed min/max with both endpoints stated; chart captions that state a computed conclusion instead of a title; `Pro (legacy) · retired` as a visible filter label.

Category-default: the Overview/Customers/Cohorts triad; the four-KPI row; the faceted filter sidebar; the nine-column sortable table at 50/page; the line-plus-diverging-bars chart pairing; the triangular cohort heatmap. Swap the typeface, add 6px radii and a shadow, and three of the four pages would be indistinguishable from any competitor's.

The material is authored to an unusual degree. The form is borrowed.

**Deterministic scan.** `detect.mjs --json src app` → `[]`, exit 0. Zero findings across 50 scannable files (27 under `app/`, 23 under `src/`).

This was verified to be a real clean sheet rather than a silent no-op: a synthetic control file carrying a purple gradient, `rounded-3xl`, `shadow-2xl`, `animate-pulse` and an emoji was placed in the same tree and correctly returned `ai-color-palette` with exit 2. The scanner fires; the codebase gives it nothing.

Independent axe sweep across 7 pages at 1280×900 **and** 360×780: **0 violations in all 14 cells**. Zero silent tab stops, zero missing focus rings, zero focusable elements inside `aria-hidden`, zero sub-24px targets. `unreachableOverflow` and `unfocusableScrollers` both empty at both widths. `npm test`: 67 passed, 0 failed, 34 skipped — the skips are the DB-backed suites self-skipping on `ECONNREFUSED 127.0.0.1:5434`, an environment fact, not a product defect.

Colour discipline, measured rather than assumed. `--color-data-2/3/4/neg` appear only inside `<svg>`. `--color-data-1` has two uses outside SVG: the `::selection` background at `globals.css:143`, and the cohort-cell backgrounds and legend swatches, which are HTML but are data marks in substance. The `@theme` comment says "If one of these appears outside a chart or a signed number, it is a bug." By its own rule, `::selection` is the one genuine leak.

**Visual overlays.** None. No user-visible overlay was rendered and no screenshot was taken — there is no native browser tool in this session. Script injection did genuinely succeed over CDP (mutable preflight passed: `document.title` writable, an appended `<script>` executed and persisted), but `<script src="http://localhost:PORT/detect.js">` was blocked on all four routes by Chrome's loopback CORS policy against an HTTPS origin, and the fallback was to inline the same bytes. The in-page detector reported `gradient-text` on `body` on all four routes and five `line-length` findings on the detail route.

The `gradient-text` finding is a **confirmed false positive**. Live computed style on `body` is `background-clip: border-box`, `background-image: none`; the served HTML — which inlines all app CSS in a single `<style>` tag — contains zero occurrences of `background-clip` and zero of `linear-gradient` on every route; the only mention of gradients anywhere in source is the comment "no gradients" at `globals.css:9`. Eight `linear-gradient` strings appeared in the DOM only *after* injection, from the detector's own 399KB of source. The `line-length` findings (~111 chars) are a real measurement of the Detail column of the events table, but the rule targets running prose and the element is a data-table cell.

## Overall Impression

This is a build where the deterministic layer is exhausted. Two independent scanners, an axe sweep at two widths, 101 tests and a positive-controlled detector return essentially nothing. Every problem left is a design judgment problem, which is the correct place for a signed-off project to be.

What remains is one shape: **the build is composed for a desktop reader who has already decided to stay, and its audience is a phone reader who has not.** The single best screen in the portfolio — the customer statement — sits behind 1,247px of filter panel on a phone. That is the biggest opportunity, and it is a composition problem, not a code problem.

## What's Working

**The customer statement.** Facing Debit and Credit columns separate revenue lost from revenue gained without using colour, which is exactly the constraint the build set itself. The `<tfoot>` is a real `<tfoot>`, so it is announced as one and survives horizontal scroll. The double rule is `border-style: double`, which renders as two hairlines with a gap, because that is what it is. Blank cells rather than `£0.00` stop every row reading as two entries. The standfirst then makes the claim testable — the foot reconciles to the headline MRR by inspection — and it does.

**The chart accessibility model, which costs nothing at runtime.** `role="img"` with a shape-describing label, `aria-hidden` internals so 200 rects are not announced one by one, the SVG stretched with `preserveAspectRatio="none"` while labels stay HTML at a real font size, `vector-effect="non-scaling-stroke"` so a stretched viewBox does not draw vertical rules thicker than horizontal ones, and the exact figures in a real `<table>` behind a native `<details>` that is keyboard-operable, findable in-page, and works with JS off. Verified reachable by Tab at stop 6 and toggling on Enter.

**The client-JavaScript budget as a design decision.** Two files in the entire build carry `'use client'` — the error boundary, which must, and the nav, which needs `usePathname` for `aria-current`. Everything else, including a nine-column sortable filtered table over 4,000 rows, is a plain `<form method="get">` and server-rendered HTML.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] On a phone, `/customers` buries the table under the filter panel.** Measured at 390×844: the filter form is 1,247px tall and the first customer row sits at 1,622px — 1.92 screens down. The skip link targets `#main`, which is *above* the panel, so it does not help. The thing the build is proudest of is two full swipes below the fold on the device the audience is holding.
*Fix:* below `sm`, wrap the panel in `<details>` with a summary reading `Filters — 3 applied` (`activeFilterCount` already exists), closed by default. Give the form `action="/customers#results"` so Apply lands on the rows.
*Suggested command:* `/impeccable adapt`

**[P1] Ticked filters and the result line contradict each other.** The count renders from applied state and sits above the panel; the checkboxes show pending state. Tick Enterprise, Active and United Kingdom and the page says "4,000 customers match — no filters applied" while three boxes are visibly checked. Nothing indicates Apply is required. A visitor who ticks boxes, sees no change, and misses the button 900px below concludes the filters do not work — on the build whose whole pitch is that they work in the database in 14ms.
*Fix:* render an applied-filter summary as removable chips directly under the result count, each a link to `customerHref(options, {…minus that one})`. That answers "what is on?" and "take one off" in one component and closes the recognition gap at the same time. Move Apply directly beneath the search field.
*Suggested command:* `/impeccable clarify`

**[P2] The headline KPI row misaligns on every phone width, and the largest figure outgrows its column.** Measured on the live page: value baselines occupy 3 distinct vertical positions at 390, 360 and 320 (1 at 1280) because "MONTHLY RECURRING REVENUE" wraps and "ACTIVE CUSTOMERS" does not. `£3,439,147` needs 141.6px against a 140px column at 360 and a 120px column at 320, spilling 21.6px of the 32px gutter.
*Correction to Assessment A, which filed this as P1 on the grounds that `overflow-x: clip` makes the spill unrecoverable:* I tested that directly and it does not hold. At 320 the figure intrudes into the gutter but stays 10px clear of the next column, and at 200% text zoom the layout viewport widens to 596px and the row fits. This is a crowding and alignment defect, not content loss. It is P2.
*Fix:* `grid-cols-1 sm:grid-cols-2 lg:grid-cols-4`, and `grid-template-rows: subgrid` on the row so label, value and note baselines align when labels wrap at any width.
*Suggested command:* `/impeccable layout`

**[P2] Three pages that never link into each other's data.** The cohort grid shows the Jul 2025 cohort at 80% in month 12 and offers no way to see those customers, though `/customers` accepts `from=`/`to=` on signup date and would express that view exactly. "Revenue churn 1.7% — 57 accounts lost" does not link to `?status=cancelled`. Both links off the overview are unparameterised. The build's headline architectural claim is that the URL is the state, which makes each of these a one-line `customerHref` call.
*Fix:* make every cohort row header a link to `/customers?from=…&to=…`; make "57 accounts lost" and "2,931" links to the filtered views they describe.
*Suggested command:* `/impeccable shape`

**[P3] Four small claims the code does not honour.** Two of the four overview KPIs print the identical `+£83,063` — once as the MRR subtitle, once as the Net movement headline — so a quarter of the headline row restates another cell. `Skeleton`'s doc comment says `rows` is "set to the number of rows the page will actually render, so the fallback is the height of the thing replacing it"; all four `loading.tsx` files pass `rows={16}`. The `/customers` masthead rule uses a different token from the other three pages. `Figure` was deliberately unified and then given two different breakpoints.
*Fix:* replace the Net movement figure with something the row does not already say (opening balance, or expansion vs churn); set real row counts; align the token and the breakpoint.
*Suggested command:* `/impeccable polish`

**Not a P0.** The previous snapshot's P0 — no database fallback on `/customers` and `/customers/[slug]` — is fixed and verified. Assessment A proposed a new P0, that `force-dynamic` against a suspending Neon free tier makes the cold link's first paint an apology. **I tested it and it is refuted:** after 6.7 minutes with no traffic, two cold requests to production returned TTFB 0.110s and 0.131s, HTTP 200, with real figures and no fallback. A's evidence came from a local server that was serving the outage page for an unrelated reason — `.env` points at a local Postgres on port 5434 and the Docker daemon is not running on this machine. The caveat worth keeping: this establishes behaviour after ~7 minutes idle, not after hours.

## Persona Red Flags

**Alex (impatient power user).** Pagination has no page number — `⇤ First / ← Previous / Next → / Last ⇥` and a "51–100 of 4,000" sentence, so page 40 of 80 is 40 clicks or a hand-edited URL. Every filter change is a document load with a scroll reset, so ticking four boxes one at a time is four round trips and four scrolls back. Neither chart has a hover readout, a plotted point, or an end-value label, so reading June 2026's MRR means opening a disclosure and scanning 24 rows. No multi-column sort, no column hiding, no density control. `Rows per page` lives inside the filter form, so he cannot change it without an Apply. Net outcome: he finds *Download all 4,000 as CSV* in about thirty seconds — well placed, well labelled, and it exports the filtered set rather than the visible page — and finishes in Excel. A good escape hatch and a lost user.

**Sam (accessibility-dependent).** The measured picture here is unusually strong and should be said plainly: 0 axe violations at both widths, every colour pairing verified by a tool that fails the build, pattern fills so hue is redundant on the bars, a printed percentage in every cohort cell so shade is redundant, one `:focus-visible` rule declared once, `aria-sort` on the sorted column only, `aria-describedby` for facet counts so the count is not read as part of the label, and a skip link that genuinely moves focus into `#main` (verified true on all 7 pages). What is left: six focusable `Scroller` regions add a tab stop each even at widths where nothing scrolls — the component's own doc comment concedes this; the cohort grid is roughly 30 rows × 26 columns of announced cells with no summary row and column headers that are bare integers (the unit is in the sr-only `<caption>`, so it is available, but never repeated once you are inside the grid); the `role="img"` chart labels are 50–60 word single sentences, so getting the shape means listening to a paragraph and getting a value means separately finding a disclosure; and the pending-filter contradiction reaches her too, with "what is on" still 23 checkboxes away after Apply.

**Creative director, iPhone, cold link, between meetings (project-specific).** The page loads fast — 110ms TTFB cold, verified — so the first screen is real. It is `Overview`, then four lines of grey 14px prose whose subject is that the company is invented. The disclaimer outranks the product on the one screen that has to sell it. She sees the KPI row misalign before she reads either number: three different baselines at 390px because one label wraps and the others do not. She taps *Customers*, the page the README is proudest of, and meets 1,247px of checkboxes; the first data row is 1.92 screens down and she will not swipe. She taps *Cohorts* and reaches the first cell at 706px, which is better than it looks but still most of a screen of methodology first. She never sees the statement page — the one screen that would have won her — because it is reachable only by scrolling past the filter wall and tapping a company name.

## Minor Observations

- Submitting the filter form produces `?sort=mrr&dir=desc&q=&plan=enterprise&status=active&country=GB&from=&to=&mrrMin=&mrrMax=&perPage=50` — every empty field and every default serialised — on the page whose lead sentence is "Copy the URL and you have copied the view." `customerHref` produces the clean form; the form does not, and the form is what a visitor copies.
- The *Recent activity* standfirst explains a Postgres index (`event (customer_id, occurred_at desc)`) inside a customer's account page, where every other standfirst on the site explains the data. This is the portfolio leaking through the product, on the page a visitor is most likely to screenshot.
- `--color-data-1` sets the `::selection` background, which is the one measured violation of the build's own "data marks only" rule.
- The h1 and the headline figure are both `text-2xl`, so "Overview" and `£3,439,147` compete for the same rank; below the h1, everything is 14px or smaller. Three type sizes carry five information levels.
- React's streaming scaffolding leaves empty `<table hidden>` elements in the served HTML — 22 on `/`, 43 on `/cohorts` — which survive into the DOM. They are `hidden` so axe is silent, but `querySelectorAll('table')` returns 44 on a page with one real table.
- `/cohorts` streams its 42 rows individually, which is why its raw HTML is 474KB and its wire size is 21KB.
- The blank Debit/Credit cells in the statement are correct ledger practice and are not explained; the standfirst covers which column is which but not why half of every row is empty.
- The footer disclaimer repeats, at the foot of every page, what that page's lead paragraph already said at the top.
- Assessment A described `/cohorts` as "two full phone screens of methodology before a single cell". Measured, the first cell is at 706px on a 844px screen — 0.84 screens. Real, but smaller than stated.

## Questions to Consider

1. Every number here is frozen at 2026-07-31 and the cold path is 110ms. What is `force-dynamic` actually buying on `/` and `/cohorts`?
2. The statement is the one screen that could not exist in a competitor's product. What would the **overview** look like drawn as a statement too — opening balance, movements, closing balance, carried forward — instead of a KPI row and two charts? You would lose the category's shape and gain the product's.
3. The URL is the state. Why can no cell of the cohort grid and no figure on the overview produce one?
4. On a phone, why does the filter panel exist above the table at all? Would three pre-built views as links — *UK Enterprise*, *Churned this quarter*, *Highest MRR* — do more for a two-minute visitor than thirty controls?
5. The prose is the best writing in the build. Would it be more persuasive as marginalia beside the data than as a wall stacked above it? A printed analytical report puts its notes in the margin, not in the lede.
6. You removed a non-functional `aria-live` because "an ARIA attribute that cannot fire is a claim, not a feature." What is the visual-layer equivalent — something that looks like a decision but does no work? The KPI row with a duplicate figure in it is one candidate; `rows={16}` on four different pages is another.
